East Arlington for NAS Jax Value Buyers

East Arlington is one of the first places we look when NAS Jax buyers need a realistic entry price without giving up a workable commute.

Our take on the value entry point

This is a practical market: solid for commute and budget, uneven on housing condition. Our job is sorting the streets and subdivisions that hold up from the ones that only look affordable on paper.

What the data will not tell you

  • Proximity to NAS Jax is the headline, but Intracoastal-adjacent pockets and inland streets are different products. We keep those comps separate.
  • Many homes are mid-century. We budget inspection findings honestly so a low list price does not become a cash pit after closing.
  • HOA vs. no-HOA choices change resale and rental flexibility — important for military families who may PCS again.
  • For Mayport, beaches may fit better; for Kings Bay, we usually look north. East Arlington shines brightest for NAS Jax.
  • When buyers outgrow value inventory, we map a clear next step into Southside, beaches, or Clay without restarting the search from zero.

Community Snapshot

East Arlington is part of the larger Arlington region of Jacksonville, which has no separate census count for the eastern portion.

Place type

Part of the Arlington region

Arlington is one of the large regions of the consolidated City of Jacksonville.

Joined Jacksonville

1968

Arlington was brought into the city through the Jacksonville city-county consolidation.

Bridge connections

Several

Arlington has bridge connections to the beaches, the Northside and downtown.

Walk Score

51 (Arlington)

Per walkscore.com in 2026, for Arlington as a whole.

Construction eras

Mid-century and later

Arlington is known for mid-century modern architecture, including homes by Robert C. Broward, Taylor Hardwick and William Morgan.

Known for

Fort Caroline & Jacksonville University

Both are in Arlington, along with the St. Johns River frontage.

Housing Overview

Many East Arlington homes are mid-century, and the broader region is noted for mid-century modern design. Intracoastal-adjacent pockets and inland subdivisions are different products, as are properties with or without homeowner associations. Pros & Considerations

An honest look at both sides.

What stands out

  • Mid-century architecture, including homes by named local architects.
  • Multiple bridge and expressway connections.
  • Fort Caroline and the Timucuan preserve within the region.
  • A variety of subdivisions and association structures.

Worth weighing

  • Condition of older homes varies, so inspections matter.
  • Pockets near the water differ from inland streets in flood exposure.
  • Commercial corridors are spread out rather than centered in one district.
  • HOA and no-HOA subdivisions can behave differently on resale.
