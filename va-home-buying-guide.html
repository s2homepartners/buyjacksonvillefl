VA Home Buying Guide for Northeast Florida

Straight answers to the questions VA buyers ask most, from new construction and seller concessions to sight-unseen purchases during a PCS. For loan-specific eligibility and terms, always confirm with a qualified VA lender.

Last updated September 2026

How does a VA loan work in Florida?

A VA loan is a mortgage benefit for eligible active-duty service members, veterans, and some surviving spouses, guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs and issued by a private lender. In Florida, VA loans are used the same way as anywhere else: eligible buyers can typically finance up to 100% of the purchase price with no down payment and no monthly private mortgage insurance. Exact terms, rates, and eligibility depend on your lender and your Certificate of Eligibility (COE), so the loan-specific details should always be confirmed with a VA-experienced lender.

Can I buy a new-construction home with a VA loan?

Yes, VA loans can be used to purchase new-construction homes, including many production and semi-custom homes built by Northeast Florida builders. The home generally needs to meet VA minimum property requirements and pass a VA appraisal. Builder-specific financing programs and preferred-lender incentives vary, so it's worth asking early in the process whether a particular builder or community has experience working with VA buyers.

Can a seller pay VA buyer closing costs?

Yes. Sellers can agree to pay some or all of a VA buyer's closing costs as part of the negotiated offer, and VA guidelines also allow the seller to contribute toward certain seller concessions up to a set percentage of the sale price. Whether a seller agrees to cover costs is part of the offer negotiation, not guaranteed by the loan program itself — your agent negotiates for it, and your lender confirms what VA rules allow in your specific transaction.

What is a VA assumable mortgage?

Many VA loans are assumable, meaning a qualified buyer can potentially take over the seller's existing VA loan, including its interest rate, rather than originating a brand-new mortgage. This can be valuable when the existing rate is lower than current market rates. Assuming a loan still requires lender approval and a qualification process, and there are implications for the original borrower's VA entitlement, so this is a conversation to have directly with a VA-experienced lender before counting on it as your financing plan.

Can I use a VA loan more than once?

Yes, VA loan benefits can generally be used multiple times over a service member's or veteran's life, including while a previous VA loan is still outstanding in some cases, depending on remaining entitlement. How much entitlement you have available for a new purchase depends on your history of VA loan use and is something your lender calculates based on your Certificate of Eligibility.

What inspections should a VA buyer consider?

Beyond the VA appraisal, VA buyers in Florida typically benefit from a general home inspection, a wind mitigation inspection (which can lower insurance premiums), and a 4-point inspection on older homes (roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC), which many Florida insurers require. Depending on the property, a termite/WDO inspection and a roof-specific inspection are also common. These are separate from, and in addition to, the VA appraisal.

What's the difference between the VA appraisal and a home inspection?

A VA appraisal determines the home's market value and confirms it meets VA minimum property requirements; it protects the lender and the VA loan guarantee, not the buyer's overall interests. A home inspection is a separate, more detailed evaluation the buyer chooses to order, covering the condition of the roof, systems, and structure. Passing a VA appraisal does not mean a home has no issues worth knowing about — that's what a home inspection is for.

Can I buy a home during a PCS before arriving in Florida?

Yes, this is common. Buyers under PCS orders regularly complete the entire home search and purchase process remotely, using video walkthroughs, a local agent who tours homes on their behalf, and a lender and title company that can coordinate a remote or mobile-notary closing. Power of Attorney can also be arranged through base legal if a service member can't be present for closing.

Can I purchase a Florida home sight unseen?

Yes, sight-unseen purchases are a routine part of military relocation. A local agent can provide detailed video walkthroughs, discuss floorplans and room sizes, and answer questions about the neighborhood and commute, standing in for an in-person showing. Many families still choose to schedule a final walkthrough before closing when their timeline allows.

How does buying with a VA loan work when stationed at NAS Jacksonville, Mayport, or Kings Bay?

The process is the same VA loan process used anywhere, applied to Northeast Florida's market: get pre-approved with a VA-experienced lender, work with a local agent to identify neighborhoods near your gate, submit an offer (often with negotiated seller concessions), complete inspections and the VA appraisal, and close, in person or remotely.
