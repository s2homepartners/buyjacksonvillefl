Northeast Florida Buyer Guide

First-Time Buyer Financing Guide

Down payments, closing costs, seller concessions, and credit guidelines — everything you need to understand before you make an offer in Northeast Florida.

Down Payments

Myth vs. Reality

Most buyers overestimate what they actually need to put down. Here's what's really required across common loan types.

Reality

0% Down

Available through VA loans (military/veterans) and USDA loans (eligible rural/suburban-fringe properties). No down payment required, though closing costs still apply.

Reality

3–3.5% Down

FHA loans require 3.5% down; many Conventional programs allow as little as 3% for qualifying first-time buyers. On a $350,000 home, that's roughly $10,500–$12,250.

Myth

"You need 20% down"

20% avoids monthly mortgage insurance, but it is not a requirement to buy. Most first-time buyers put down far less — the trade-off is a PMI/MIP line item until enough equity builds up.

Closing Costs

What You'll Actually Pay at Closing

Typically 2–4% of the purchase price in Florida. Here's what's usually included.

🏦

Lender Fees

Loan origination, underwriting, application, and credit report fees charged by your mortgage lender.

📄

Title & Escrow

Title search, title insurance (owner's & lender's policies), and the closing/settlement agent's fee.

💰

Prepaid Taxes & Insurance

Your lender collects an initial deposit into escrow for property taxes and homeowner's (and flood, if applicable) insurance.

📋

Florida Documentary Stamps

State excise tax on the deed and, in most counties, on the mortgage note — a Florida-specific closing cost line item.

Seller Concessions

Lower Your Out-of-Pocket Cash

A seller concession is a credit the seller agrees to pay toward your closing costs, negotiated as part of the offer. Each loan program caps how much you can use.

Conventional

3–9%

Scales with down payment size

FHA

6%

Of the purchase price

VA

4%

Plus normal/customary costs

USDA

6%

Of the purchase price

We regularly negotiate seller-paid closing costs into offers so buyers bring less cash to the table — ask us how in your specific scenario.

Qualifying Guidelines

Credit Scores & Debt-to-Income

General benchmarks lenders use — your specific rate and terms depend on the full financial picture.

Typical Minimum Credit Score

VA Loan ~580–620+
FHA Loan ~580+
USDA Loan ~640+
Conventional ~620+

Debt-to-Income (DTI) Benchmarks

Under 36% Strong position
36% – 43% Typical qualifying range
43% – 50%+ Program-dependent, needs compensating factors
