San Marco for Walkable Move-Up Buyers

San Marco is the boutique, walkable play south of downtown — restaurants, parks, and homes with character for buyers graduating out of a first starter property.

Our take for move-up buyers

San Marco fits buyers who will trade yard size and new-construction warranties for location and lifestyle. We pressure-test insurance, updates, and true walkability to the spots you will actually use.

What the data will not tell you

  • Move-up buyers often underestimate renovation timelines. We help you decide between turnkey and project houses before you underwrite the dream.
  • "Walkable" depends on your block. We map grocery, parks, and dining against the specific addresses on your shortlist.
  • Condo and single-family dynamics differ sharply here — HOA health and special assessments matter as much as the kitchen remodel.
  • Downtown and Southbank job centers make this a commute win; Mayport does not. We keep base-driven buyers oriented correctly.
  • When San Marco inventory is thin, we show smart alternatives in nearby historic pockets without pretending they are the same product.

Neighborhood & Community Guide

San Marco

Brick-paved squares · Riverfront parks · 1920s architecture

Duval County · Prepared by S² Home Partners · Last reviewed October 2026

2

Community Snapshot

A neighborhood rather than an incorporated town, so there is no separate census count. These are the facts that can be sourced.

Place type

Neighborhood of Jacksonville

Part of the consolidated City of Jacksonville in Duval County, south of downtown across the St. Johns River.

Boundaries

I-95 to the river

Commonly described as bounded by I-95, Philips Highway, Greenridge Road and the St. Johns River.

History

Developed from 1925

Part of South Jacksonville, incorporated in 1907 and annexed into Jacksonville in 1932; development of the neighborhood began in 1925.

Walk Score

75 (ZIP 32207)

Transit Score 38 and Bike Score 59 for the same ZIP, per walkscore.com in 2026.

Construction eras

1920s onward

Original 1920s–30s building stock with later infill, townhomes and condominiums.

Known for

San Marco Square

The Art Deco San Marco Theatre (1938), the Acosta Bridge (1921) approach, and a count of 14 neighborhood parks.

3

Why People Love Living in San Marco

A square to gather around

San Marco Square is the neighborhood’s center, with brick-paved streets that radiate out from it and cafes and restaurants within a short walk.

Theatre and film

The San Marco Theatre dates to 1938, and Theatre Jacksonville’s Little Theatre is part of the neighborhood’s performing-arts footprint.

Riverfront green space

Parks including Balis Park, plus the Southbank Riverwalk just north along the river, keep open space close to the street grid.

4

Neighborhood Personality

What the place itself feels like to move through.

Square-centered

Shops, dining and a theatre are clustered around San Marco Square, which makes it a natural starting and ending point for the day.

Mature streetscape

Brick-paved streets and a tree canopy give the older blocks a settled, quiet look away from the main avenues.

River-adjacent

The St. Johns River forms the neighborhood’s edge, and water-view and river-access blocks differ from the interior streets.

Weekend rhythm

A weekend farmers market at Balis Park and a theatre calendar give the square a regular schedule of activity.

5

Housing Overview

San Marco’s housing stock was largely built from the mid-1920s onward, so Mediterranean Revival and Colonial Revival homes and Craftsman bungalows sit alongside later infill. Townhomes and riverfront condominiums add a second housing type, and the mix changes quickly from one block to the next.

1920s–30s

Mediterranean Revival, Colonial Revival and Craftsman bungalows built during the neighborhood’s first development.

Mid-century infill

Smaller post-war homes on interior streets.

Townhomes & condos

Newer attached homes and riverfront condominiums.

Older-home upkeep

Roofs, systems and insurance underwriting deserve extra attention on pre-war construction.

Sale prices here move too quickly to print in a guide, and every block behaves differently. Ask me for this week’s numbers on the specific streets you are considering.

12

Pros & Considerations

An honest look at both sides.

What stands out

  • Square-centered layout with dining, coffee and a historic theatre close together.
  • Distinct 1920s architecture and mature street trees.
  • A short drive to downtown Jacksonville.
  • Parks and a weekend market within the neighborhood.

Worth weighing

  • Older homes can mean higher upkeep and insurance questions, so a thorough inspection matters.
  • Lot and parking sizes are modest on many blocks.
  • Flood zone and elevation vary by block near the river; check each address.
  • Drive time to Mayport is longer than from the beach communities.

13

Local Insider Tips

Park once

Park near the Square and cover the dining and shops on foot rather than driving between them.

Go early to the market

The Balis Park market is easiest to enjoy when you arrive near the start.

Check flood maps

Look up the FEMA flood zone for each address you are considering.

Budget for older systems

Ask about roof age, wiring, plumbing and the insurance carrier’s requirements.

Check the theatre calendars

Both the San Marco Theatre and the Little Theatre post their own schedules.

Walk the Riverwalk at sunset

The south-bank riverfront faces downtown’s skyline.

A Weekend in San Marco

  1. Morning

    Coffee at Southern Grounds & Co., then the Balis Park market if it is a weekend.

  2. Midday

    Browse the boutiques around San Marco Square.

  3. Afternoon

    Walk the Southbank Riverwalk or visit the Museum of Science & History.

  4. Evening

    Dinner at Bistro Aix or The Grotto, then a show at the San Marco Theatre.

14

Final Thoughts

San Marco rewards a visit on foot: the Square, the theatre, the parks and the river are all within a short walk of each other. If the features above match what you are looking for, we can show you the specific blocks and homes where they line up, and tell you what to check on older construction.
